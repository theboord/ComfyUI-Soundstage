# Stage API for tools

Methods on the 3D stage (`web/stage/stage.mjs`, the `Stage` instance the App keeps as `node.ssStage`) for tools that edit a scene without a mouse: ✦ Ask's actions, scripts, tests. `duplicate`, `placeInFrame`, `setRig`, `setPose`, `setExposure` and `restoreSnapshot` are ordinary edits: on success each re-poses the stage and records one undo step (through `onChange`), like doing it by hand; a refused call changes nothing and records none. `openPhysicsPanel` only selects and opens a panel (no scene data changes), so it records no undo step, as selecting by hand records none. None of them throws for a bad argument (including `null` or a missing `where` for `placeInFrame`): they say what was wrong.

| Call | Does | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| `stage.duplicate(id)` | A copy of object `id` (person, prop, vehicle) beside it: offset along +X by its height x 0.8 (at least 1 m), with its **base** keys (`keys`, deep-copied, offset the same), so in every shot without blocking of its own it moves like the original. Its per-shot blocking (`by_shot`) is not copied, even when the active shot has some. Does not need or change the selection. | the new id, or `null` when there is no object `id` |
| `stage.placeInFrame(id, {x, y?, distance_m?})` | Puts the object's ground point at a place in the **active shot camera's** frame (the shot's keys, rig and lens at the playhead, never a live virtual-camera preview on screen), on the walkable ground: the stage floor or the set's own raised floor (a station platform), so the point lands on the third at that floor's height. A person's feet follow the floor by themselves (key height 0); a prop on a raised floor is keyed at that floor's height (`key_y` set), and `key_y` is dropped only when none of its keys in any shot is raised any more. `x`: `"left"` or `"right"` (the thirds, 1/3 and 2/3 of the width), `"center"`, or a number 0..1 (0 = left edge), also as a numeric string (`"0.3"`); a boolean, an array or an empty string is refused. Riding (`mount_id`) or carried (`carry_id`) objects are refused (place what holds them). Under a steep or top-down camera the ground distance first falls then grows up the frame (the column passes under the camera): every crossing is found and the one nearest the frame's centre line is used, so near distances work there too. `distance_m`: how far from the camera, measured along the ground. `y` (0..1, 0 = top), used when there is no `distance_m`: where that screen point meets the ground. With neither, it keeps its present distance. A one-key object moves as a whole; a keyed (moving) one gets a key at the playhead. With per-shot blocking (`by_shot`) in the active shot, that shot's keys are edited and the base `keys` / `position` stay. | `{ok: true, position: [x, y, z]}` (y: 0, or the raised floor's height for a prop) or `{ok: false, error}` (unknown id, `where` not an object, bad `x` / `y` / `distance_m`, a point above the horizon, a distance nearer than the ground the camera sees, or farther than the camera can put it: a steep or top-down camera sees ground only so far up the frame, and the error says how far). `where` omitted or `null` = `{x: "center"}` at the present distance. |
| `stage.setRig(type, amount, params)` | Puts the active shot's camera on a rig (`free`, `tripod`, `dolly`, `chapman`, `slider`, `jib`, `steadicam`, `handheld`, `drone`, `drone_fpv`); `amount` 0..2, `params` `{seed, arm, pivot}` as before. | `{ok: true, rig}` (a copy of what was set), or `{ok: false, error: "unknown rig '<type>'"}` (also for inherited names like `toString`), or `{ok: false, error}` when `params` is given and isn't a plain object (a string, number, array); nothing changes then |
| `stage.openPhysicsPanel(id?)` | Selects object `id` when given, then opens the Physics panel (`physpanel.mjs` `open()`) for the selection. The App hands its panel to the stage as `stage.physicsPanel`. | `{ok: true, id}` (the selected id), or `{ok: false, error}` (unknown id, no panel, or `{ok: false, error: "select something"}` with no id and nothing selected: no empty panel is opened) |
| `stage.setCameraCurve(mode)` | The active shot's camera curve: `"smooth"` (curve version 2, [camcurve.mjs](camcurve.mjs): no speed jumps at keys, focal in log space), `"even"` (version 2 at one even speed by arc length, camera and target travel combined; interior key times and holds are ignored) or `"legacy"` (the old curve shots saved before version 2 play). One undo step. `stage.cameraCurve()` reads it. | `{ok: true, curve}` or `{ok: false, error}` |
| `stage.setPose(id, pose, opts?)` | Sits or stands a person (man, woman, child). `pose`: `"sit"` on a seat: `opts.on` = the seat's id, else the nearest **free** seat within 1.5 m (ground distance) of where they are at the playhead, **real seats first**: a chair, bench, sofa or armchair anywhere within 1.5 m is picked ahead of a nearer table, cube or rock (those only when no real seat is near); nearest within each group. **One person per seat** (a sofa or bench too: the seat is one point, so a second sitter is refused, not placed beside the first). **A seat too high is refused:** the seat top, measured **from the floor** (world Y: the prop's own height in the world plus its seat height, less the set's walkable floor under it, else 0; `_seatHeightFromFloor`), at most `max(0.8 m, 0.6 x the person's height_m x scale)` (1.08 m for a 1.8 m adult: a 1 m cube is fine, a 3 m cube, a chair at `scale: 4` (seat 1.88 m) or a chair stacked on cubes 1 m up (`key_y`, seat 1.47 m above the floor) is not). **A seat that moves with someone is refused:** one that is carried (`carry_id`), mounted / riding on something (`mount_id`), or on the sitter themself through a chain of mounts / carries; a seat someone sits on is not picked up from under them (dropping it on a person does nothing). The seat height comes from the prop (humans.mjs `MOUNTS`: chair 0.47 m, bench 0.47, sofa 0.54, armchair 0.44, table 0.77, rock 0.6, cube its height, scaled by the prop's `scale` and its `height_m` against the library height; a named static custom prop: 0.6 x its height). It is the same mount as dropping them on the seat (`mount_id`, `pose: "sit"`). `"sit_floor"`: down on the floor where they are (`pose: "sit_floor"`, hips 0.10 m up, knees up); from a seat, they get off it and sit on the floor at their own keyed position. `"stand"`: up again (off the seat, or up from the floor). **The person's movement keys (`keys`, `by_shot`) are never changed by any pose:** standing up puts them back on their own keyed path (a walk 0 to 8 m is still that walk after sit, stand, sit_floor), so after standing they are where their keys say, which can be inside the seat they left when a key is there. The lifelike body follows in both (people/avatars.mjs `sitBody`): it is not sent to the clay. **`sit_floor` is refused while the person has movement keys** (base `keys` or any shot's `by_shot` keys at two different spots; a hold, every key at one spot, is fine), so a seated body never slides along a keyed path: remove the movement keys first, or sit them on a seat. Movement keyed after they sat (a later key edit, an older save): neither the clay nor the lifelike body sits while the active shot's keys move (`_sitSpec` returns null, the clay walks the path upright); the pose is kept and they sit again once the keys hold still. One undo step when something changes. | `{ok: true, pose, on?: seatId, changed}` (`changed: false` = already so, no undo step), or `{ok: false, error}` with nothing changed: `no object '<id>'`, `'<name>' is not a person`, `unknown pose '<p>' (sit, sit_floor or stand)`, `opts must be an object like {on}`, `'<name>' is being carried`, `'<name>' has movement keys (<base / shot ids>): sit_floor would slide them along the path; remove the movement keys first, or sit them on a seat` (sit_floor only), `no object '<on>'`, `can't sit on '<name>' (a chair, bench, sofa, armchair, rock, table, cube or a static prop)` (a car, horse, bike, boat, a person, a non-seat prop such as a crate), `'<seat>' is taken by '<name>'`, `can't sit on '<seat>' is being carried by '<name>'`, `can't sit on '<seat>' is mounted on '<name>' (it moves with it)`, `can't sit on '<seat>' is on '<name>' (a seat can't ride on the person sitting on it)`, `'<seat>' is too high to sit on (seat <h> m above the floor, at most <max> m for '<name>')`, `nothing to sit on within 1.5 m of '<name>' (none free and low enough): pass {on: id}, or use 'sit_floor'` |
| `stage.setExposure(stops)` | "Lights down": the scene dimmer, in photographic stops, `-6`..`+2` (`-1` = half the light, `0` = as lit; `EXPOSURE_MIN` / `EXPOSURE_MAX` exported). Every light in the set (sun, sky fill, film lights, practicals, headlights, fire) and a plain-colour sky are scaled by 2^stops while the viewport renders (any view mode, and the shot-camera picture-in-picture, camviz.mjs `PipViewer`), then put back. **Captures are not dimmed to near-black:** a Color capture (`capture`, `captureSequence`, `renderFrameTo`: what Frame, Motion and Deliver read) is dimmed by at most `CAPTURE_EXPOSURE_MIN` (-2 stops, a quarter of the light; exported), and the guide passes of a capture (clay, depth, wire, ID, dust: the clay guide Frame and Motion send) are not dimmed at all; the prompt's lighting line carries the rest of the darkness; emissive surfaces (neon, screens) keep their glow. Saved in the scene as `lighting.exposure_stops` (absent at 0); the prompt's lighting line says "lights dimmed down, low-key exposure" (-1 or lower), "lights down very low" (-3 or lower) or "lights up bright" (+1 or higher). Numbers or plain decimal strings (`"-1.5"`; not `"1e3"`), rounded to 0.01. One undo step when it changes. `stage.exposure()` reads it (a number, 0 when unset). **A saved value is clamped:** `load()` (which an agent `set_lighting` op goes through, agent.mjs `loadScene`) clamps `lighting.exposure_stops` to -6..+2 and drops a non-number (`true`, `"1e3"`, `"-2"`, NaN); `exposure()`, the render factor and the prompt line read it the same way (`exposureStopsOf`, lighting.mjs, exported). | `{ok: true, stops, changed}` (`changed: false` = it was already that, no undo step), or `{ok: false, error}` with nothing changed: `stops must be a number (got '<x>')` (null, booleans, arrays, `""`, `"1e3"`, NaN, Infinity) or `stops must be between -6 and 2 (got <n>)` |

Tested in [tests/js/avatars_m15.test.mjs](../../tests/js/avatars_m15.test.mjs) ("Stage API for tools"): `placeInFrame` against a known camera (the projected point lands on the third at the asked distance, within 2 mm), a straight-down camera refusing 10 m, and `null`; `openPhysicsPanel` recording no undo step ("round 2" tests); `placeInFrame` with a preview camera on screen and with per-shot blocking, `setRig` with bad `params` and inherited names, `duplicate` of a person with per-shot blocking ("round 3" tests).

More in [tests/js/people_m16.test.mjs](../../tests/js/people_m16.test.mjs): near distances under a top-down and a steep camera, numeric strings and refused booleans / arrays, riding or carried objects refused, a raised platform floor (the point lands on the third at the platform's height; `key_y` kept for another shot's raised keys), and `openPhysicsPanel()` with nothing selected.

**Play is read-only.** Playing a shot (the App's Play button: `stage.setFrame(f)` every tick), scrubbing, the key strip (`keyInfo()`) and the viewport overlays that run on every render (camviz.mjs `shotPathSig`, `CameraViz._rebuildPath`, `TopView` drawing, tracks.mjs `TrackSheet._rows`, objparts.mjs `_camKeyFrames`) never write `shot.camera_keys` and never write the flat `state.camera_keys` field: they read through `_camKeysView()`, and a shot with no keys yet plays and draws from the scene's camera without a key being stored (the live scene stays byte-identical). Edits still store keys: `_camKeys()` (TopView insert / drag, a Tracks drag of the fallback key, every key tool) creates the first key. The flat `camera_keys` field (read by older readers such as soundstage/look/scene.py, which the Look panel's `stage.toJSON()` feeds) is written into the **saved copy** only: `stage.toJSON()` always puts the shot **on screen** there: a copy of the active shot's keys, or for a shot with no keys yet the one key it films from (`[{frame: 0, ...camera}]`, what `_camKeysView()` plays), never a previous shot's keys left in the live field (after `setActiveShot('b')` and a play the saved scene is shot b's), so a preset that hands a shot a new keys array is still seen by those readers; the live `stage.state.camera_keys` is moved only by an explicit key edit (`_camKeys()`: `setKeyHere`, `setFocal`, an orbit / TopView drag, the key tools), never by Play, scrubbing, `setKey('start'|'end')` (First / Last, a jump: it reads `_camKeysView()`), `setActiveShot` (viewing another shot) or the preset hover preview. **Opening a scene writes no keys either:** `load()` (which jumps with `setKey('start')`) stores no key on a keyless active shot and creates no flat field; an older scene with no `shots` gets one Master shot that shares the flat field's array, or takes its `camera` / `camera_end` move as two keys, or (a still camera) no keys at all, and the flat field is not created. Tested in [tests/js/askhooks_play.test.mjs](../../tests/js/askhooks_play.test.mjs) and [askhooks_overlays.test.mjs](../../tests/js/askhooks_overlays.test.mjs) (every case fails on the pre-hook code, e3774ad).

## Ask's host hooks (web/soundstage.js `mountAsk` host, helpers in web/hosthooks.mjs)

| Hook | Does | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| `host.project()` | The open project, read now from its saved `project.json` through `window.__ssProject.project()` (web/project/projectbar.mjs). Async. Never throws and **always settles**: a read that has not answered in 3 s (`PROJECT_TIMEOUT_MS`; `projectHook(getCtl, {timeoutMs})`, where a `timeoutMs` that is not a finite number >= 0 or a numeric string is the 3 s default, never 0 ms) resolves with `id: null`. | a Promise of `{name, id}`: `id` is `"p_" + 8 hex` only when confirmed against the saved project.json, else `null` (not saved yet, saved before ids existed, a copy, a renamed or reused name, a timed-out read); a read that threw gives the tab's own sync id (`projectId()`); `name` the project's (or the tab's) name, or `null` |
| `host.cut.panel()` | The live `CutPanel` instance (web/cut/cut.mjs), the very object the Cut view uses, so its own methods can be called (and its edits go through its own Undo). The App builds the panel at start, hidden: `panel()` returns it only while the Cut view is **in the page and shown** (the Cut view is open: `cutView.hidden` false). | the panel, or `null` when the Cut is not on screen (another step is open, or the view left the page): open the Cut first with `host.cut.show()` (shows the Cut view, as the strip's "Open editor" does; there is no pipeline step for it) |
| `host.lights` | `{get(), set(stops), down(stops = 1), reset()}` on `stage.setExposure`: `down(n)` dims n stops more from where it is (n >= 0, a number or a plain decimal string) and **stops at -6** (-5.5 then `down(1)` gives -6; at -6 it is `{ok: true, stops: -6, changed: false}`), `set(stops)` is absolute (out of range refused), `reset()` back to 0. To take a change back, use `host.stage.snapshot()` / `restore(snap)` (below): `host.lights` has no undo of its own. | `get()`: a number; the others: what `setExposure` returns; `down` with a negative or non-number n (`""`, `null`, `true`, `"1e3"`, NaN, Infinity): `{ok: false, error: "stops must be a number >= 0 (got '<n>')"}`; no stage: `{ok: false, error: "no stage"}` |
| `host.stage` | `{toJSON(), select(id), setPose(id, pose, opts), snapshot(), restore(snap), undo()}` (web/hosthooks.mjs `stageHook`). `setPose` is `stage.setPose` (above). **Taking an edit back:** `snapshot()` before the edit returns the whole scene as an opaque string (`stage.snapshot()`: `{soundstage_snapshot: 1, epoch, scene_ids, scene}` as JSON, stamped with the scene open now); `restore(snap)` puts it back (`stage.restoreSnapshot(snap)`) as **one new undo step**, after checking the **whole** snapshot first: the stamp, that it was taken in the scene open now (the stage's scene epoch, which `resetHistory()` moves on when the App opens another scene or workflow, and the shots' `scene_id`s), and a complete scene (actors with unique ids and valid positions / keys, camera position and target, `shot.frames` >= 1 and `fps` > 0, shots with unique ids and valid camera keys, `active_shot` one of them, lighting an object). A refused restore changes nothing (state, undo, meshes); a scene the set can't be built from is rolled back. `select(id)` refuses an id not on the set (the selection stays); `select(null)` clears. **No stage** (`stageHook(null)`, a getter that returns null or throws): nothing throws, `toJSON()` / `snapshot()` give `null`, `select` / `setPose` / `restore` give `{ok: false, error: "no stage"}`, `undo()` `{ok: false}`. so the person can still Undo Ask's restore. A restore puts back the whole scene: an edit the person made between the snapshot and the restore is taken back too. `undo()` is the stage's own Undo: it takes back the **last** step, whoever made it, so prefer snapshot / restore. | `snapshot()`: a string (`null` with no stage); `select(id)`: `{ok: true, selected}` or `{ok: false, error}` (`no object '<id>'`, `no stage`); `restore(snap)`: `{ok: true, changed}` (`changed: false` = the scene already was that) or `{ok: false, error}` with nothing changed (`snapshot must be the string stage.snapshot() returned`, `not a snapshot (bad JSON)`, `not a snapshot (pass the string stage.snapshot() returned)` (a bare scene such as `'{"actors":[]}'`), `not a snapshot (<reason>)` (`no camera (position and target)`, `no shot timing (frames, fps)`, `bad shots list`, `shot '<id>' has bad camera keys`, `active shot '<id>' is not one of its shots`, `two objects with id '<id>'`, ...), `snapshot is from another scene (...): not restored`, `snapshot could not be restored (<why>): nothing changed`, `no stage`); `undo()`: `{ok: true}`, or `{ok: false}` with nothing to undo |

The Ask model server (`soundstage/assistant.py`, `GET /soundstage/assistant/status`) also says `local` and `url_kind`. **`local` means "the model server's URL is a loopback address or this machine's name", not "the data stays on this machine":** `local: true` for `127.x.x.x`, `::1`, `localhost`, `0.0.0.0` or this machine's own host name (never resolved through DNS), `false` for any other host. An SSH tunnel (`localhost:11434` forwarded to a pod) reads `local: true` although the model runs on the pod: the server cannot see a tunnel. `url_kind` tells the URL cases apart: `"loopback"` (a server here or a tunnel; the two cannot be told apart), `"this_host"` (this machine's own name) or `"remote"`. `POST /soundstage/assistant/chat` sizes Ollama's `num_ctx` to the request: (its messages + its `tools` list as JSON) / 3.5 tokens, + 1536 tokens per image in a message (`IMAGE_TOKENS`), + the reply room (`options.num_predict`, else 2048), **snapped up to one of a few fixed sizes (`CTX_SIZES`: 8192, 16384, 32768, 65536, 131072)**, never below the server's context (`OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH` as the ComfyUI process sees it, else Ollama's default 4096) and never above `SOUNDSTAGE_ASSISTANT_MAX_CTX` (default 32768, raised to the server's context if lower): a short chat and a ~3,500-token planner prompt both get 8192. **The value is stable, because Ollama reloads the model whenever `num_ctx` changes:** the chat route reuses the largest `num_ctx` it already sent to that model on that Ollama URL (`_ctx_sent`, in this ComfyUI process; a short chat after a 16384 call sends 16384 again), never lowers it, and forgets it only when `unload()` frees the model or ComfyUI restarts (`forget_ctx(model)`). The request's own `options.num_ctx` (an int, a float or a plain decimal string) is a floor, snapped up to the same sizes (12000 -> 16384, 20000 -> 32768), even above `SOUNDSTAGE_ASSISTANT_MAX_CTX`, and is never lowered. **Every number is bounded by `CTX_CEILING` (131072):** the caller's `num_ctx`, `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH` and `SOUNDSTAGE_ASSISTANT_MAX_CTX` are clamped to it (`1e12`, `1e308`, `"1000000000000"` give 131072); NaN, Infinity, exponent strings (`"1e3"`), hex, booleans and other non-numbers are ignored; what is sent to Ollama is always a whole number in 4096..131072. Tested in tests/test_assistant.py (including the chat route's wiring).
