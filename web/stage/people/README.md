# Lifelike people (Microsoft Rocketbox avatars)

Men and women on the 3D stage can show as lifelike people instead of the clay figure. The clay figure is still there underneath and runs everything (walk, keys, gaze, mounts, mocap, Drive); only its meshes are hidden while the lifelike body shows. Children and everything else stay clay for now.

## Getting the files (they are not in the repo)

The avatars and clips come from [Microsoft Rocketbox](https://github.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox), MIT licence, Copyright (c) 2020 Microsoft, pinned at commit `0943055db6ec570bcef9f2c8b41c9e5467c808f9`. Only fetch from that commit or a later one: before 2020-11-17 the library had a non-commercial licence.

They are kept out of git (about 27 MB). For now a maintainer puts them in `web/stage/people/rocketbox/` by hand; a one-click download from the pinned commit comes later. The folder holds:

| File | From the Rocketbox repo |
|---|---|
| `Male_Adult_01.fbx`, `Female_Adult_01.fbx` | `Assets/Avatars/Adults/<name>/Export/<name>.fbx` |
| `tex/m002_*.png`, `tex/f001_*.png` (body / head colour, normal, specular, and the opacity map; the specular maps are not used and never downloaded) | the avatars' `Textures/*.tga`, shrunk to 1024 px PNG with macOS `sips`; the opacity PNG keeps its alpha |
| `anim/m_idle_neutral_01.max.fbx`, `m_walk_…`, `m_run_…`, `m_gestic_talk_…`, `f_idle_…`, `f_walk_…`, `f_run_…` | `Assets/Animations/all_animations_max_motextr_static/` and `_xy/` |
| `LICENSE.md` | the MIT licence, copied next to the files |

Without the folder every person silently stays clay (one line in the browser console says so).

## How it works

- `avatars.mjs` loads each avatar once (FBXLoader; every `.tga` the FBX names is loaded as `tex/<same name>.png`, because three's FBXLoader has no TGA reader here), keeps only the most detailed mesh, swaps its Phong materials for the Standard material the rest of the set uses, and clones it per person.
- The body is scaled by its measured standing height (top of the head in the idle pose), so it stands as tall as the person is set to (the woman's hips sit higher than the clay's, so hip height can't be used).
- The body plays its own Rocketbox clip: idle when still, walk, run, crossfaded by ground speed over about half a second. Walk and run share ONE gait phase that advances with the distance travelled over the stride of the clips as mixed, so a walk-run blend stays in step and the feet don't skate; idle runs on the shot's frame clock. The pose depends only on the frame number, so renders are frame-exact.
- Only the steady ground travel of each clip (on the root bone `Bip01`) is removed: the straight line from its first key to its last. The sway and surge within a stride and the idle's weight shift stay. The clips' own planted feet still slid 5-9 cm/s; the foot grounding below plants them.
- The body is lowered by the **playing clips' own floor** (each clip's median standing sole height over its loop, blended by the clip weights), not only by the idle's first frame: the run's planted foot stood 2 cm into the floor before.
- A capture (sprite sheet) waits for bodies still loading (up to 60 s, `stage.avatarWaitMs`; past that it goes ahead, says in the status line who stayed clay, and `stage.lastCaptureNote` keeps the message), and a body that finishes loading during a capture goes on after it, so a capture is never part clay, part lifelike. A failed load is not remembered: the next rebuild of the stage tries again.
- A rebuild of the stage (changing the set, an appearance, adding someone) keeps each person's lifelike body when their avatar model is unchanged, and many people attaching at once cost one re-pose, not one each.
- Depth, ID, Clay and Wire passes and the dust matte all draw the posed body. The hair and lash cards keep their cut-out in every pass (`cutout.mjs`: the card swaps in a version of the pass material with the card's alpha and alpha test, which the pass's override material can't replace), so they never show as solid quads. The underwater set's own Depth material is the same skinning shader. The Pose pass reads the lifelike body's real bones while it shows.
- The physics lift (an unbaked dynamic or static object raised to its start height) moves the lifelike body with the clay.

## Foot grounding (the feet on the ground)

Measured before this (try-it demo, real GPU render): planted feet slid 5-9 cm/s, the idle's right foot hovered 3-5 mm, the idle-walk crossfade sank the feet up to 26 mm into the floor for 4-5 frames, and a turn on the spot swivelled the whole body over sliding feet. [`footplant.mjs`](footplant.mjs) fixes that on the avatar's **own skeleton** (the Rocketbox Biped legs: `Bip01_L_Thigh` / `_Calf` / `_Foot` / `_Toe0`, the pelvis root `Bip01`); it never reads the clay figure, so it works whatever drives the visible body (also once one motion drives both the avatar and the hidden logic rig).

- **Sole points, once per avatar** (bind pose): the lowest shoe vertex of each patch of each sole, plus the shoe's lowest point toward every way the foot tips (heel strike, toe off, a roll) out to the toe cap and the back of the heel: 33-41 points per foot, only vertices the foot and toe bones carry alone. The real skinned vertices are measured, not the bones: the toe bone sits 1.5-7 mm above the sole. The rig keeps the sole's up and forward axes in the foot bone; on the Rocketbox Biped forward is the bone's local +Y and up its local -X, so nothing may walk the sole along a fixed local axis.
- **One plan per person per shot** (cached on the skin like the gait table, rebuilt when the keys, the shot length or fps, the size, the set, the parked props, the height table or a dock / deck table change): the clip is played at every frame; a foot is in **stance** while its sole is within 2.5 cm of the **body's own footing** (the clip's contact, whatever the ground under it does) and its **contact speed** (the slowest sole point within 1 cm of the lowest, with the body's own turn and climb taken out) is under 0.3 m/s, kept up to 4 cm / 0.45 m/s; both speed limits widen by 0.15 per m/s of body speed and a 2-frame stance counts from 2.6 m/s, so runs and sprints plant. Scaled by height. The ground is `stage._supportAt` (the stage floor, a set's raised floor such as the station's platforms and ramps, set boxes and parked props) or the dock / deck height a person stands on over the water.
- **Planted frames roll:** each keeps the clip's own foot turn (heel strike, flat, heel off, toe off) tilted by the ground's slope, and the patch of sole points touching the ground is **pinned** frame by frame outward from the stance's middle, so the sole rolls over its contact without sliding. Each frame is lowered until the sole point nearest the ground under it touches. Under an edge or a step (the ground under some sole point more than 2 cm off the sole's plane: heel, toe and side found along the foot's own axes) the foot stands level on the highest ground under it, so toes over the platform edge rest on it.
- **The reach guard:** a planted frame the posed hips can reach only with more than 2 cm of pelvis drop is not planted, unless it is the only foot the clip has down (then up to 6 cm: a short dip on a stair instead of both feet in the air); nor is a sole more than 30 cm from the body's own footing. Blends the leg can't reach are weighted down.
- **Turning on the spot:** a long stance whose foot would twist or drift too far is broken into **steps**: one foot at a time, only while the other is planted, lifting 5 cm, over 0.3 s or quicker when the body turns fast (at least 4 frames, the body turning 0.6 rad a step). A planted foot turns half the body's turn, at most 0.07 rad a frame, on its ball with the heel lifted. A clip foot dragged along the floor between two plants (the idle-walk blend's shuffle) is a lifted step too.
- **Between plants** the foot moves up or down by the ground, from the last plant's offset to the next's (a swing from one stair to the next, from a hollow to a bump), faded out next to a jump. A moving foot that is not planted clears the ground under every sole point by 1.5x its distance from its plant (at most 3 cm, in full at 0.3 m/s), so a sole touches only where it plants.
- **Per frame**, after the clips are posed: each foot's target is the plan's, blended with the clip's own foot (moved by the ground) by a 3-frame ramp in and out of every plant (1 frame at a sprint); the **pelvis comes down** (up to 12 cm) when a planted foot is out of the leg's reach; **two-bone IK** turns the thigh and the calf so the foot lands on its target, keeping the knee on the side the clip bends it to; a foot not planted is lifted clear of the ground under every sole point.
- **The gait keeps pace:** in the idle-walk blend (0.15-0.6 m/s) the walk's stride is divided by its weight, so the stance foot no longer creeps forward under a slow walk (it did 5 cm/s at 0.5 m/s, and the hips dipped 7.5 cm to reach it); a person keyed to face away from where they travel walks backwards (the phase runs back), feet planted.
- **Who is grounded:** the gate is the feet (`avatarcore.feetUpReason`), not the clay: riding or seated, carried, moved or lifted by physics, knocked over / tipped / lying, swimming or floating, and frames in the air are not grounded, and no plant blends across them. Holding or carrying a prop, gaze or Pose keys, a mocap take, being driven live or Blocking look are grounded. Today everyone who shows as clay is still not posed lifelike at all; once the clay is retired, the gate is what decides.
- **Deterministic:** the plan is built from the whole shot at once, and a frame reads only the plan and the clip pose at that frame. The clip's leg values are put back before the mixer poses the next frame (three's `PropertyMixer` writes a bone only when the clip's value changed, so posing the same frame again kept the IK's values). Building costs about 20 ms per person for a 5 s shot (Node, this Mac); a frame after that about 0.2 ms.
- **One foot always down at walking speed** (under 2 m/s): the idle-walk crossfade drags both feet at once when a walk stops, starts again or turns round. A step is taken only while the other foot is planted: it waits planted for the other foot, lands early and waits, or the other foot waits; any frame left with both feet off is closed by a hold (with a single-support hip dip of up to 6 cm when the level hips can't reach). A foot at rest is planted, never held up by the swing clearance.
- **The ground the body stands on:** the swing clearance counts only ground the leg could step onto (none over the body's own footing for a foot at rest, up to 30 cm for a swinging one, or up to the higher of the two plants it swings between), and never folds the leg closer to the hip than 45 % of its length. A plant is refused too high as well as too low, and a body standing still plants on its own footing: keyed 2 cm off a platform's edge or a 20-40 cm ledge, the feet stay on the floor (toes or heels go into the side of the top: the keys put the body closer to it than the shoe).
- **Walked steps, not hops:** the lifelike body's height is `stage._walkHeightAt` (people walking up a 20-45 cm step, a kerb, set-box stairs), not the clay's `_heightAt` (which takes every rise as a ballistic hop and counted the frames before a step as in the air): a step's rise is taken over a quarter second, a ramp or a slope is followed exactly, and only a rise or drop over half the leg (a quarter of the height: 45 cm for 1.8 m, 27.5 cm for 1.1 m) is a jump (`_walkAirborneAt`). A step is a **break in the ground**: the path between two frames is looked at every 2 cm and only a change over 2.5 cm between two looks counts, so a slope walked or run at any speed and frame rate is followed exactly (a change of 2.5 cm per FRAME used to be the bar: 20 degrees at 1.6 m/s lagged 8-23 cm behind the slope). Keyed exactly on a set floor's edge, the footing looks 2 mm either way. Walking along a platform's edge, a planted shoe across the drop moves sideways (up to 20 cm) onto the body's own ground, and the swing follows it. It counts the set's own boxes the way the feet do. The clay's own height is unchanged.
- **Runs and sprints touch down every stride:** at a run's speed the stance's contact speed limit widens to 45 % of the body's speed by 6 m/s, a 1-frame stance is kept, the clip's own touch-down frames get no swing clearance, and a touch-down next to a plant is held there.
- **Swimming is per frame for the grounding gate** (`avatarcore.swimsAt`): a person who walks and then swims is grounded on the walk (the clay swap itself is still whole-shot). `avatars.footPlan(stage, a)` builds the plan whether or not the body shows.
- **Cost:** about 20 ms per person to build the plan for a 5 s shot, 115 ms for 30 s (Node, this Mac), 0.2 ms a frame after. A shot longer than 240 frames keeps the clip's own feet during a live drag and rebuilds when the drag ends.
- `stage.footGrounding = false` turns it off (the clips alone, for A/B checks); `skin.feet.plan` and `skin.footOut` hold the plan and the last frame's result (see [../STAGE_API.md](../STAGE_API.md)).

Measured in Node on the real files, man and woman, physically on every shoe vertex and every frame the body is on its feet ([tests/js/footground.test.mjs](../../../tests/js/footground.test.mjs) with [tests/js/footkit.mjs](../../../tests/js/footkit.mjs), and [tests/js/footplant.test.mjs](../../../tests/js/footplant.test.mjs)): standing at the real station platform's edge facing the track or away, soles on the platform with no pelvis drop (was 12.7 cm into it, hips at the cap); walks at 0.3-1.2 m/s with a foot always down, touching soles moving under 0.6 cm a frame, the pelvis within 2 cm; runs at 3-6 m/s planted on both feet; a 15 cm kerb, five 15 cm stairs, bumpy ground and the station ramp with no shoe a centimetre into anything; 360s in 1 s and 2 s stepping 3 + 3 and 5 + 5 times with planted soles under 0.6 cm a frame; walking backwards planted. Still open: the sun shadow (2048 px over +-25 m, soft, no bias) doesn't reach the soles, which was the biggest part of the "floating" look in the measurement; the stairs still have a few frames with no foot within 5 mm of a tread.

## When the clay shows instead

M1 can't yet make the lifelike body follow everything the clay can do. A person stays clay for the whole shot (never a swap mid-shot) when they ride or sit on something, are carried, carry something or hold a prop, play a video mocap take, have Pose keys or gaze keys, swim anywhere in the shot (every Swim mode, `off` included: it means Can't swim now, so they struggle and sink on the swim plan; only a video mocap take in water too deep to stand in floats at the waterline, which the body can't follow: "floats in the water"; where the water is shallow enough to stand in, auto and Can't swim wade on the bottom and the lifelike body shows), are moved by physics (a baked fall) or were knocked over (a saved tilt, from a knock-over or the Rotate gizmo, which writes the tilt as you drag), or have been driven live in this shot. Two exceptions are not whole-shot. A jump, hop up or vault (or a drop off an edge) shows the clay **only on the frames the person is in the air** (the clips have no jump: mid-air the body would walk, while the clay tucks its knees); on the ground before and after it is the lifelike body, so the body swaps at take-off and again at landing. Which frames are "in the air" is the same ballistic height table the clay uses (`stage._airborneAt`), kept up to date with the shot's fps and with any move or edit of the thing jumped onto; a crate, step or riser counts as climbable by its height above the floor it stands on (a station platform's 0.92 m floor included), not its height above the stage. The other: while a person is knocked over live in a physics drag (the live world's pose, before it is written down) they show as clay; `live.mjs` flags that from the physics pose (`markLiveTilt`), not from the clay group's rotation, so the ordinary lean of a walk or run (into turns, starting, stopping) never swaps the body. The dust matte keeps the hair and lash cut-outs too. Hover the **Face** control to see why someone is clay (re-read each time you hover).

## Controls

- **Face** (the Appearance row, with a person selected): `shown` (default) or `faceless`. Faceless keeps the head's shape and paints the face one flat skin colour: no painted brows, eyes or lips, no lashes, and the **hair stays** (see below). Saved on the person as `face: "faceless"`. The Pose pass draws a faceless person without the 68 face landmarks and without eye and ear points (the nose and neck stay, so the body still has a head).
- **Blocking** (beside Wire / Clay / Color / Depth: it is for the whole scene): every person shows as the clay figure. Only the look changes; the walk, keys and passes run the same. Saved with the scene as `people_look: "blocking"`.

### How faceless keeps the hair

Rocketbox puts hair in two places, and faceless keeps both:

- **Painted on the head texture** (`<id>_head`): most of the scalp hair (the woman's whole scalp and her ponytail roots; the man's brows are painted there too). Faceless no longer swaps the head for a plain material: it keeps the head's own texture and paints only the **face** one flat skin colour, sampled (blurred) from the avatar's own cheek. The face is a per-vertex mask (`faceMask`, `avatarcore.faceMaskAt`), computed once in the bind pose in eye spacings from the point between the eyes: from under the chin up to just above the brows (fading out before the hairline), across to the outer cheeks (before the ears and temples), and only in front of the ears. Brows, eyes (the eyeballs become skin-coloured), lips and nostrils go; the head keeps its shape, and the hair, ears and neck keep their texture.
- **Cards** (`<id>_opacity`): one material and one texture for hair strands AND lashes (in `f001` the lashes are a corner of the hair atlas; `m002`'s atlas is all hair). Skinning doesn't separate them (the woman's lower lashes ride the head bone like her hair), so `buildKit` splits the triangles by position, once, in the bind pose: a card triangle within 0.6 eye spacings of an eye bone and in front of the eyes is a lash or brow and moves to its own material `<id>_opacity_lashes` (same texture). Measured on the real files: the woman's 208 lash triangles all lie within 0.37 spacings and her nearest hair card is 3.3 spacings away; the man has no card within 0.83 spacings, so nothing of his is split (`kit.report.lashTriangles`). Faceless hides `_opacity_lashes`; `_opacity` (the hair) stays.

Checked in a browser (both avatars, close-up, Color pass): hair, ponytail and ears kept; brows, eyes and lips gone. Still rough: the flat face colour can sit a shade off the neck (the man's face reads a little greyer), and the normal map still carries the brow ridge and lip line.

The pure rules are in `avatarcore.mjs` and tested in `tests/js/avatars.test.mjs`; that test also parses the real files when they are installed.

The selection and hover outlines follow the cards too: a lifelike person's outline proxies draw each geometry group with its own material, so the hair and lash cards keep their cut-out shape and Faceless's hidden lashes stay hidden (viewport only, `web/stage/outline.mjs`). Tools that edit people from code (duplicate one, place one in frame) use the calls in [../STAGE_API.md](../STAGE_API.md).
