EXT. BEACH - SUNSET

The sun sits fat and orange on the water. Wet sand mirrors the sky.

JUNE (30s, rolled-up jeans, a camera on a strap) walks the tide line. Her dog, a scruffy terrier, runs ahead and barks at the foam.

A red SURFBOARD lies half-buried at the waterline. No footprints lead to it.

JUNE
Leave it, boy.

She kneels and turns the board over. A name is carved into the wax: CAL.

JUNE (CONT'D)
(quietly)
You never lent this to anyone.

Waves HISS up the sand and pull back.

INT. HARBOR DINER - NIGHT

A narrow diner, chrome stools, a jukebox playing something slow. Rain streaks the window.

CAL (40s, sea-worn, a bandage on one hand) sits alone in a booth with a cold coffee.

The door BANGS open. June comes in with the surfboard under her arm and sets it on his table.

CAL
That's not mine.

JUNE
It has your name on it.

CAL
Lots of boards have my name on them. I made them.

June slides into the booth across from him.

JUNE
Then tell me why this one washed up without you.

EXT. CLIFF ROAD - NIGHT

Cal's old pickup truck runs the cliff road, headlights cutting the fog. The surfboard rides in the bed.

INT. PICKUP TRUCK - CONTINUOUS

Cal drives. June watches the dark water below.

JUNE
Where are we going?

CAL
Where the board went in.

Headlights sweep up behind them. A black SEDAN closes fast.

Cal checks the mirror and floors it.

CUT TO BLACK.
