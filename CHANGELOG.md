**20:50 PST · Drive takes over from keys (a car keyed mid J-turn drives instead of sticking), kept takes are auto-corrected and fitted with far fewer keys, and ⌂ Back to the set / ↶ Last view on the viewport**
- **Still wrong (read first):** checked in Node tests and headless Chrome on a local build of the try-it page (swiftshader), not in the live App or on the pod; the ⌂ / ↶ buttons sit under the App's view badge by measurement, not by a screenshot of the App. The key fit picks which recorded frames become keys but does not solve their values (the camera's reducer does), so a fast weaving car still needs a key every 6-9 frames (a minute at 43 km/h: 1,440 frames -> about 150 keys); people, horses and steady drives come out much lower. - **Root cause** ([web/stage/drive.mjs](web/stage/drive.mjs), [web/stage/vehsim.mjs](web/stage/vehsim.mjs)): the stage already skipped a driven car's path keys, but (1) a keyed stunt pose often overlaps a kerb, wall or parked car (the stunt simulation doesn't collide), and `LiveCar.bump` took 30 % of the speed and half the yaw rate on every contact, even driving away from the wall, so a car started there could not get out: in the repro, W held for 2 s moved it 0.2 m; (2) keys still leaked into a driven object: the stunt's keyed tyre smoke kept pouring where the keys had the car, and Pose Studio keys and mocap still posed a driven person; (3) the keyed roll and pitch snapped to level on the first frame; (4) recording sampled 24 fps frames from a 60 Hz sim (2 or 3 steps apart), so a steady drive was keyed 40 % fast, slow, fast, and a slow screen frame sampled one state twice and then jumped.

HUD note "Took over from keys: Esc to leave". `bump` only takes speed off on a real hit. Esc without a take restores the keys exactly (unchanged).
- **Recording:** a whole number of sim steps per shot frame (72 Hz at 24 fps), and frames are posed and sampled inside the sim steps (`_recStep`).
- **Auto-correct and key fit** ([web/stage/takefix.mjs](web/stage/takefix.mjs), new): `limitsFor` (the live car's grip, top gear and roll gradient; a person's or a horse's top speed, acceleration and turn rate), `correctTrack` (dropped frames, teleports, out-and-back spikes, top speed, yaw spikes and turn rate, roll and pitch, ground), `reduceKeys` (replays keys as the stage does, splits the worst segment until every frame is within 2 cm and 1°, keeps mode changes and holds). `applyTake(..., {reduce})`; a take under review is already the corrected one. The HUD toast (`takeReport`: "Smoothed 3 spikes, settled roll · 1,440 keys → 38") has **Undo** (`useRaw`: the raw take, a key every 2 frames). The phone viewfinder and the desk report "frames → keys" ([web/vcam/viewfinder.mjs](web/vcam/viewfinder.mjs), [web/vcam/desk.mjs](web/vcam/desk.mjs)).
- **Lost camera** ([web/stage/viewnav.mjs](web/stage/viewnav.mjs), new): **⌂ Back to the set** (.) flies the free view to a home view that fits the set's boxes and objects in 0.4 s (the shot camera is not moved); **↶ Last view** (,) steps back through the last 10 settled views (rested 0.5 s, or marked before Drive, Back to the set, F or a compass jump); a **Lost? **18:50 PST · Houdini-grade destruction, part 1: real collision shapes, Voronoi fracture with a constraint network that bends and cracks, splintered render meshes (Milestone 27)**
- **Why:** "I want to know why the physics in Houdini is so well done and how we can make something similar, if not better." From SideFX's docs (RBD Material Fracture, Constraint Networks, Bullet): Houdini's look is (1) fracture by material, clustered at the impact, (2) a constraint network of glue with per-joint strength, where glued pieces ride as one compound body, (3) soft constraints with plasticity, so things bend and crack before they break, and (4) a cheap proxy simulated while a detailed mesh is rendered. All four are in now; Houdini isn't needed.
- **Real collision shapes** ([web/stage/convex.mjs](web/stage/convex.mjs)): quickhull (written here; three's ConvexHull isn't vendored), hulls capped at 24 vertices, exact mass properties, and a decomposition into up to 4 hulls for concave props (a chair, a table: per mesh part, merged while it adds little empty volume; a single mesh is cut V-HACD-style with one level of lookahead). Hull contacts in the XPBD bake: SAT over both hulls' faces, Bullet-style face clipping (a 4-point manifold), and GJK/EPA for edge-on-edge. Boxes keep their fast path. Car bodies collide as a raked hull (bumper, fascia, bonnet), so debris is thrown up over the bonnet.
- **Voronoi fracture with a constraint network** ([web/stage/fracture.mjs](web/stage/fracture.mjs)): a crate's boards (1-3 a face) are cut into Voronoi cells stretched along the grain (wood) or torn panels with creases (cardboard), built at the prop's first real hit and clustered round it. Joints: nails, wood fibre (stronger), creases, tears, each ±15% seeded. Fibre and creases yield before they break: a board bends (a plastic deflection pinned at its ends) and a flap folds, permanently; every knock leaves damage, so a 4.5 m/s nudge cracks and bends a crate and a later blow breaks it (progressive, not all-or-nothing). Glued groups ride as one compound hull body; broken ones fly with the parent's v + ω×r. Deterministic, baked to keys.
- **Proxy → render** ([web/stage/shards.mjs](web/stage/shards.mjs)): the solver moves clean hulls; the clay and Color guides show each cell as its boards (8 mm seams), with ragged torn edges, split-timber ridges and splinter needles on boards snapped across the grain; cardboard tears zig-zag.
